Issues We Shape

Where the room focuses its influence.

01

AI and The Future of Work

67% of Black workers already say AI is affecting their jobs today, and Black unemployment has climbed to 7.5% — nearly two points above the national rate — as AI-linked layoffs ripple through the economy. Black employees hold less than 5% of jobs at the tech and social-media companies building these tools. AI is reshaping who owns the platforms, the data, and the value they create. The question the Power Table sits with: how do we make sure Black-owned businesses become builders and owners in this shift, not just another workforce left to adapt?

02

M&A and Entrepreneurship Through Acquisition

As Baby Boomers retire, an estimated $10 trillion in business wealth will change hands over the next decade — the "Silver Tsunami," with 60% of those owners lacking a succession plan. Yet fewer than 1% of Black business owners currently engage in M&A, and Black-owned firms make up just 2.4% of U.S. employer businesses. Entrepreneurship Through Acquisition (ETA) — buying an existing, profitable business instead of starting from zero — is one of the clearest ways to change that. The question the Power Table sits with: how do we capture as much of that $10 trillion for the Black community as possible?

03

Rebuilding DEI and Black Business Support

Federal agencies once aimed to steer 11–15% of contract dollars to small disadvantaged businesses — instead, the Black-owned share of those contracts fell from 1.61% in 2023 to 1.54% in 2024. Meanwhile, Target, Meta, Amazon, and others have scaled back the supplier-diversity and DEI programs that once opened doors to capital, contracts, and mentorship. The question the Power Table sits with: how do we rebuild that infrastructure ourselves, instead of depending on any single company's willingness to keep showing up?

04

Combating Systemic Racism

The median Black family holds $44,100 in net worth — about 15% of the $284,310 held by the median white family — a gap Federal Reserve data shows has widened, not narrowed, in recent years. Black Americans hold just 3.4% of the nation's wealth despite being roughly 14% of the population, a disparity rooted in redlining and lending discrimination that persists today. The question the Power Table sits with: how much of that gap can we close, and how do we protect what we've already built?
